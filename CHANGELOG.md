# Changelog

Plain-English record of what changed and when. Newest first.

This is the human-readable companion to the git history — read this to see what happened, read the commits for the technical detail.

## [2026-08-12]

- **Your company-wide pricing defaults are now editable, and every one of them says what it reaches.** Settings has a new **Bid pricing defaults** section for default overhead, default profit method and percentage, and the productivity factor. Each carries the same plain warning: *this is your company default — it affects every new bid going forward, and every existing bid still set to follow it.* That second half is the part people do not expect, because bids **follow** these settings rather than copying them, so changing one moves bids you have already built unless that bid was given its own setting. What a change can never do is alter what a finished bid was built **from** — the materials, hours and rates frozen onto each line when it was added stay exactly as they were, and there are now tests holding that line. Overriding a setting on one bid is unchanged and stays an ordinary local edit, with no company-wide warning attached to it.
- **The bid breakdown now shows the productivity factor doing its work.** When the factor is set, the Labor line spells out the arithmetic — *"20.4 h after modifiers, +12% productivity"* — alongside the adjusted total, and says whether the factor came from your company default or from that bid. Without it the hours on a bid quietly disagree with the hours on your assemblies and the only way to find out why is to go hunting in Settings. It appears in Quick bid the same way. At 0% nothing extra is shown, because there is nothing to explain.
- **New: a productivity factor.** One number that adjusts all labour hours up or down company-wide, for how your crews actually perform against the hours written into your assemblies — positive if the work takes longer than the book says, negative if your crews beat it. It ships at 0%, does nothing until you set it, and is fully reversible: it is applied when a price is calculated and never rewrites the hours stored on your assemblies, so putting it back to 0% returns every number to exactly where it was. It is **not** another job-condition modifier and never gets added in with them — modifiers describe one job's conditions and add together, while this is applied afterwards as its own separate step, so you can always see which of the two moved your hours. You can override it on a single bid like any other setting.
- **The "where do I go?" helper now points at Claude**, the same AI the rest of the app already uses, on its cheapest tier — this is looking up which of a dozen screens you want, not heavy thinking. It had been configured for a different provider's model that the app has no account with, so it would have quietly never answered. When it cannot reach the AI it now records the reason *and which model it tried* in the server log, so a misconfiguration is something you can look up rather than guess at; on screen it still just says it isn't sure, because a missed navigation hint is not worth an error message.
- **The sidebar is now grouped into three labelled sections instead of one flat list of eleven.** **Workspace** at the top is what you open daily — Dashboard, Quick bid, Bids. **Library** in the middle is what you build once and reuse — Materials, Labor Rates, Modifiers, Assemblies, Kits, and your supplier price list. **Settings** at the bottom is what you set up and forget. When the sidebar is collapsed to icons the sections are separated by thin rules, so the three purposes read as three things whether it is open or not. Two entries are gone from the sidebar: the old **Assembly Builder**, which was superseded by Library § Assemblies and sat confusingly next to it under an almost identical name, and **Trash**, which held deleted items from the old projects system and is replaced by the bid **Archive** now shown under Settings. Both pages still exist at their old addresses, so nothing you have bookmarked breaks. **Material Database** is now called **Supplier Pricing** — it is the price list you import from your supply house, and calling it "Material Database" put it one letter away from the Materials catalog, which is a different thing entirely.
- **A new account now starts somewhere built for a first day**, rather than on the full Dashboard with every screen showing at once. It asks for one thing — what an hour costs you — and then puts you straight into a Quick bid. Just one step, and you can skip it, but it says plainly what skipping costs. Labour is asked for first because it is the number that multiplies *every* line of a bid: a few unpriced materials understate a job slightly, whereas a $0 labour rate leaves out the entire labour cost while the bid still looks finished.
- **Starter labour rates now ship at $0, flagged "Needs rate"**, the same way materials do. They used to carry plausible round figures, and a plausible number nobody chose is indistinguishable on screen from one you set. Rates you have already entered are untouched.
- **A getting-started checklist on the Dashboard**: set your labour rates, price your first material, build your first assembly, complete your first bid. It ticks from your actual data, never from having visited a screen — a checklist that congratulates you for opening a page is how someone ends up believing they are set up when they are not. Close it whenever you like and bring it back from the same spot; it retires itself once all four are done.
- **A "where do I go?" helper.** Type a plain question — "where do I edit labor rates" — and it points you at the screen, with a button to go there. It picks from a fixed list of real screens rather than making up a destination, so it can never send you somewhere that does not exist, and if it is unavailable it just says so and gets out of the way.
- **Materials are now listed by size, smallest to largest, within each category.** They used to be alphabetical, which is close to meaningless for this trade: it files 1" EMT between 1/2" and 1-1/4", and puts #1/0 wire next to #1 instead of above it. Wire is the awkward case and it is now handled explicitly — American Wire Gauge counts *backwards* (18 is thinner than 14, which is thinner than 1), then flips at 1/0 and counts upwards again through 4/0 before turning into kcmil. Sorted as plain numbers you get 1, 2, 3, 4, 10, 14; sorted as text you get 4/0 sitting between 4 and 6. Both of those look sorted on screen, which is exactly why they are dangerous — you scan for the next size up and find the wrong row. The correct order is now written out as a table rather than calculated.
- **The Materials screen stays fast at 600+ items.** It only builds the rows you can actually see — about twenty at a time instead of all 619 — so scrolling, searching and filtering stay immediate as your catalog grows.
- **Track lighting and its heads are separate items now**, because a section of track does not light anything on its own and a takeoff listing only the rail has forgotten most of the cost. Track comes in 4', 6' and 8' sections. **Under-cabinet lighting is split the same way**: rigid bar fixtures at 12", 18", 24" and 36" sold per unit, and LED tape light sold by the foot, because pricing a cut-to-length reel as a single unit is wrong by an order of magnitude either way.
- **Cartridge fuses are their own item**, at every amperage of fused disconnect in the catalog — 30, 60, 100, 200, 400 and 600A. A fused switch ships empty and the fuses are a real cost that gets replaced later; folding them into the switch as an option is how they vanish from a takeoff.
- **Crimp lugs are now sized the way they are actually sold** — by the range of conductor they accept (14-10, 8-6, 4-2, 1-1/0, 2/0-4/0 AWG) rather than one row per gauge. The old per-gauge rows implied a precision that does not exist and left you hunting for a "#3 lug" that no supply house stocks.
- **The duplicate 6" wafer light is gone.** The 5"/6" wafer already fits both trim openings, so the two rows were the same product listed twice — a choice between a part and itself.
- **The starter catalog now has about 600 materials instead of 29.** Wire and cable down to the individual size (THHN solid and stranded, aluminium feeder, all nine NM-B sizes, nineteen MC sizes, UF-B, SER/SEU, bare copper ground); five conduit families — EMT, PVC 40, PVC 80, rigid and IMC — at every trade size from 1/2" to 4", each with its connector, coupling, 90 and LB; flex and liquidtight; strut by profile with its straps, nuts and all-thread; boxes in plastic and steel; the full breaker, panel, meter and disconnect range; lighting; grounding; life safety; fasteners; connectors and consumables; the residential equipment a job connects at the end; and placeholder rows for commercial gear. Low-voltage material — Cat6, coax, speaker and control wire, landscape lighting — is filed under its own trade rather than mixed into the electrical list. **Nothing already in your library was duplicated:** of the 29 materials that were there, 26 kept their names and their rows exactly as they were, and three were renamed in place to fit the families that have grown up around them (`1/2" PVC` is now `1/2" PVC Sch 40`, and the two EMT connectors read size-first like the 224 fittings beside them). Renaming in place rather than replacing matters because your assemblies, kits and takeoff counts point at those rows.
- **Every starter material now costs $0, and the app says so.** The old catalog carried plausible-looking estimate prices, which was the more dangerous choice it looks like the opposite of: a stale guess is indistinguishable on screen from a price you checked, so a bid could be built, sent and won on numbers nobody ever verified. An unpriced material now reads **Needs price** where the figure would be, and a **Needs pricing** filter on the Materials screen shows only those, with a count — so "my catalog is unpriced" becomes a list that visibly shrinks as you work through it rather than a vague worry. Prices you have already put on your own copies are untouched, and stay untouched on every restart. One honest consequence: a starter assembly's material cost is $0 until you price its parts, so its labour hours carry the whole figure.
- **You can record the brand or part number you actually buy.** The catalog is deliberately generic — "Wire nuts", not one manufacturer's part, because a shipped catalog naming one brand is wrong for everyone who buys another — so each material now has an optional note for the one you order.
- **Searching "romex" returns the cable again, not the staples that hold it.** Found while checking search quality against the bigger catalog: the word was recorded in two places at once, which was enough to lift an accessory above the product it serves. The same class of mistake as the old "recep" bug, and now covered by tests that check ranking across the whole catalog rather than item by item.
- **Strut channel nuts are now in the starter catalog**, and searching "spring nut" finds them — along with "unistrut", "kindorf", "superstrut" and the thread sizes. The item was not there at all before, so there was nothing for the alias to attach to.
- **A material you add by hand can now carry its own trade slang, and the app will suggest it.** Until now the add form only asked for a name, category, unit and cost — the trade-slang field existed in the database but nothing on screen ever filled it in, so a material you added yourself was findable by the common abbreviations everyone shares and invisible to the specific words people actually say for it. Every starter material has that slang; yours did not. There is now a field for it, plus a **Suggest** button that proposes the terms an electrician would type. Suggestions arrive switched off and you tick the ones that are right — nothing is added on your behalf, because a wrong alias quietly spoils every future search for that item. Anything the suggestion offers that names a _different_ material in your catalog is thrown away before you ever see it.
- **Trade slang can now be edited on a material you already have, not just set when you add one.** The field and its Suggest button now appear when you edit any row in the Materials library, so a term that was typed wrong, missed, or never added can be fixed — previously slang could only be set at the moment a material was created, which left a bad or missing term permanently wrong on a row you could otherwise change freely. This works on starter materials too: editing one gives you your own copy as it always has, and the copy keeps the words you chose. Clearing the field is treated as a real decision and stays cleared, rather than being quietly refilled from the starter list the next time the app restarts.
- **Layers.** Two colour-coded checklists in the takeoff panel: **System** (what a thing is — Devices, Lighting, Panels, plus conduit and cable runs) and **Location** (where it sits — Underground, Slab/Floor, Wall, Ceiling/Overhead, Exposed, Roof). They filter independently and combine, so "just the devices, and just the ones in the wall" is one setting rather than a choice between two. Turning a layer off hides it both on the drawing and in the counted-items list at the same time, and the panel says plainly when you are looking at part of a sheet — a filtered takeoff that looks complete is how a job gets quoted missing half its receptacles. Everything starts switched on, and anything new that appears arrives visible.
- **Where something sits is tagged on the item, not the assembly.** A receptacle is always a Device, on every job — but the same receptacle can be in a wall here and a ceiling there, so Location is set on the placed mark or the traced run. You can tag one at a time, or tag every stamp of one assembly on a sheet in a single action.
- **Legend capture, finished properly.** The browser pop-up that asked for a symbol's name is gone. Capturing now means dragging a box around the symbol on the drawing; the crop becomes a small picture stored beside the entry, and the naming happens in a proper on-screen form that behaves like the rest of the app. Legend entries are now recognisable at a glance rather than by name alone.
- **The stamp tool.** Pick an assembly once, then click every place it occurs on the plan — no re-selecting between clicks and no quantity to type. Each click drops a marker and the count climbs on its own. Markers are deliberately plain high-contrast rings rather than imitations of the drawing's own symbols, so you can see at a glance what you have already counted against what you have not. Click any marker to select it and remove it if you misclicked.
- **A live counted-items list.** Everything you have placed or traced on a sheet, in one panel: stamped assemblies with their quantities, and traced runs with their footage. Every entry has numbered chips — click one and the drawing rings that exact marker, so finding the third of twelve receptacles is a click rather than a hunt.
- **A legend that remembers.** Capture a symbol off the plan's legend and name it. The first time you click it, it asks once which assembly it matches; every click after that loads that assembly straight into the stamp tool. **Links are kept against your account, not the job**, so a symbol linked on one set of plans is already linked on the next one — which is the whole point. Choosing the assembly uses the same search as the Assembly Builder.
- Stamps get the same protection tracing already had: clicks are queued and saved in batches, mirrored in the browser as you go, and anything clicked but not yet saved is recovered and re-sent when you come back.
- **You can now trace conduit and cable runs on a plan and get real footage.** Pick "Trace conduit" or "Trace cable", click along the route, and the length updates as you move — finish with Enter or a double-click, undo a point with Backspace, back out entirely with Escape. Each finished run appears in the right-hand panel with its footage, and the bid's running totals sit underneath.
- **Conduit and wire are counted separately, and deliberately so.** A conduit run counts its pipe **once**, no matter how many circuits go down it. Every circuit you assign to that run gets its **own full length of wire** for each of its conductors — three circuits of three wires in a 100 ft pipe is 100 ft of conduit and 900 ft of wire, not 300 of either. Cable runs (Romex, MC) count as cable only, with no conduit line at all, because the cable is its own raceway. Getting this wrong in either direction costs real money, so the two numbers are never combined anywhere.
- **Nothing can be measured on a sheet without a scale.** If a sheet has no scale, or the drawing itself says "not to scale" and you have not overridden it by hand, the tracing tools simply are not offered and the drawing says why and where to fix it. A run traced before a scale was set is kept and stays unmeasured — it counts for nothing until you set one, rather than quietly contributing a wrong number. If you change a sheet's scale after tracing, affected runs say so rather than silently re-pricing.
- **Trace work is protected from the moment you start.** Progress saves to the server every few seconds while you are tracing and mirrors to the browser on every click, so a crash, a closed tab or a reload does not cost you a forty-click route. You are warned before leaving with unsaved progress, and an interrupted trace is offered back when you return to that sheet.
- **Deleting from your library no longer loses anything.** Removing a material, assembly or kit used to hide it with no way to get it back — it looked like a soft delete but behaved like a permanent one, because nothing in the app could show you what had been hidden. All three now work the way Modifiers already did: removing asks first, says plainly that the item is being **archived and not deleted**, and puts it in an **Archived** tab you can restore from. There is no time limit — unlike an archived bid, these are small records with nothing attached, so they keep indefinitely. Deleting something for good is a separate action available only from the Archived tab, with its own warning that it cannot be undone. Removing a modifier now asks first too, which it previously did not.
- **You can now see just your own library items.** Every library screen has an **All / Mine** switch with counts, so you can look at only what you have created or customised instead of hunting for your two edited prices among twenty-eight shipped ones. On the labelling audit that prompted this: all five screens were already correct — an unmodified shipped item says "Starter", a starter you have edited says "Your copy", and something you built from scratch says "Yours" — but the rule was written out five separate times, so it now lives in one place with tests pinning the direction.
- **Fixed a way to lose a bid by mis-clicking.** On the Bids list, the small trash-can button archived the bid the instant you clicked it — no question asked, no undo offered, and nothing on the page to say where it had gone. It never deleted anything permanently, so nothing was ever actually lost, but a stray click made a bid vanish from the list with no explanation. It now asks first, in the same words the Dashboard uses: the bid moves to the Archive and is permanently deleted in 30 days unless you restore it, and nothing about it changes meanwhile. The icon is an archive box rather than a trash can, because it does not delete. Confirming shows an **Undo** for the mistake that gets through anyway, and an **Archive** button now sits at the top of the Bids list — as it already did on the Dashboard — so archived work is one click from anywhere you can archive from. Checked at the same time: there is no way anywhere in the app to permanently delete a bid in one step. Permanent deletion exists only inside the Archive, only for something already archived, and the server refuses it otherwise.
- **Plan PDFs can now be up to 150MB, and you can watch them upload.** The old limit was 30MB, which turned away most scanned commercial sets. Raising the number alone would not have worked: files were being sent through the app server, and the hosting platform caps any single request at 32MB, so in practice nothing much over ~24MB ever made it regardless of what the app said. Plans now upload straight to storage from your browser, which removes that ceiling entirely. Because the browser is doing the sending, you get a **real progress bar** — a percentage and "82.1MB of 150MB", not a spinner that leaves you guessing whether a ten-minute upload has frozen — and a Cancel button. Uploading several sets sends them one at a time, so the first is usable while the rest arrive. If a file is too big or is not really a PDF despite its name, it says so in plain words and the message stays on screen until you dismiss it, rather than vanishing with a toast.
- **The plans screen now works like a real takeoff workspace.** It is a split view: the sheet index down the left, the drawing in the middle, and a reserved panel on the right that the counted-items list will fill in a later phase (it says so, rather than sitting there blank). You can drag the divider to give either side more room. **Sheets have real names** — where the PDF carries bookmarks, which most architectural sets do, the index shows "E1 - Power Plan" instead of "page 1"; where it does not, every sheet still gets a "Sheet 3" label you can rename, so you are never left clicking through blank page numbers to find the panel schedule. A name you type sticks, and reopening the drawings never overwrites it. Clicking any sheet jumps straight to it.
- **Each sheet carries its own drawing scale.** The app reads the scale off the sheet where it is stated plainly, shows it in the toolbar marked **Detected** so you always know where it came from, and counts how many sheets are scaled at the top of the index. Where a sheet states two scales — a plan and a blown-up detail, which is common — it deliberately does **not** pick one, because a wrong scale would make every measurement wrong by a constant amount with nothing looking broken; instead it offers what it found as a one-click suggestion. Setting the scale by hand is always available whether or not anything was detected, either by typing it (`1/4" = 1'-0"`, `1" = 20'` and `1:100` all read correctly) or picking from the common list. Scales are stored per sheet, so a site plan at 1" = 40' and a detail at 3/4" = 1'-0" can sit in the same PDF.
- **Bids can carry plan PDFs now** — the first piece of the rebuilt takeoff screen. Open a bid and there is a **Plans** button beside the due date; from there you can drop as many PDFs onto the job as it has sheets, or click to pick them, and read through any of them without leaving the app. The sheet list down the side shows every plan with its page count and size, and paging works with the arrow keys, Page Up/Down, and Home/End as well as the on-screen arrows, with the page number shown as "3 / 18" so you can see how big the set is before you start. Measuring and takeoff tools are not here yet — this phase is about getting the drawings onto the bid and being able to read them.
- **Bids can be archived, and the archive really does empty itself.** Archiving takes a bid off the dashboard without deleting it — useful for a job that is finished or one you have stopped chasing — and it works at any status, so you never have to mislabel a job as Lost just to get it out of the way. Archived bids sit in a new **Archive** screen with the number of days left and the actual date they will be deleted, soonest first, and a Restore button that puts everything back including its plans. After 30 days they are deleted permanently, for real: a nightly job now does the deleting. The old Trash screen counted down to a deletion that never happened, which meant the number on it was decoration; this one is enforced.
- Fixed the plan renderer, which had stopped working after a library upgrade and was quietly falling back to drawing PDFs on the main thread — the thing that makes the app freeze for several seconds on a dense drawing.
- Pressing Enter in the labor-rate pencil now saves the rate **and** closes the little panel, instead of saving it and leaving the panel sitting open for you to click away from. Escape does the same — throws the edit away and closes. Editing a rate is one keystroke to finish now, not a keystroke and then a click somewhere else. Typing a column of quantities on a bid is unchanged: Enter there still saves and stays put, ready for the next figure.

## [2026-08-11]

- You can now change a labor rate straight from the Assembly Builder — a small pencil next to the role opens the rate right there, no trip to the Labor Rates screen. It says plainly that you are editing the one shared rate used across your whole library, not a rate for that assembly alone, and tells you how many assemblies will reprice. Bids you have already built keep the rates they were priced at.
- Fixed a serious bug this uncovered: editing one of the built-in labor roles quietly dropped the labor cost to **$0** on every assembly using it. Editing a built-in role makes your own copy of it, and assemblies were still pointing at the original — which then went into hiding, so nothing was found and the labor priced at nothing. Assemblies now follow the copy correctly. This affected assembly and kit costs; bids that had already frozen their costs were never at risk.
- Added the **Dashboard**, which replaces the old Projects page as the app's home view. It shows every bid in four columns — Draft, Active, Won, Lost — with each bid's name, value, deadline and line count on the card, so you can size up where everything stands without opening anything. Draft and Active sort by deadline with the soonest first, and bids with no deadline set drop to the bottom of those columns rather than crowding out the ones that have a real date. Won and Lost sort by whatever you touched most recently. Overdue and due-today deadlines are colour-coded, and the header shows what you have out for bid in total. **Bids now have a due date** — optional, since not every job has a firm deadline — which you set from the bid itself. Clicking any card opens that bid.
- Five changes that make building assemblies faster. **Small parts come with a sensible count** — add wire nuts and you get 3, not 1, because nobody fits a single one; same for straps and connectors, and every one is still just a starting number you can change. **Any assembly can be duplicated**, starter or your own, giving you a separate copy to modify — unlike editing a starter, which quietly makes it _your_ version of that starter; a duplicate is its own thing and editing it never touches the original. **The materials you use most appear first** in the Assembly Builder before you type anything, so the same dozen parts are one click away. **The whole add-a-material flow works without the mouse**: type, arrow to pick, Enter to add, the quantity is waiting and selected, Enter again and you're back in the search box for the next one. **And there are now Kits** — named bundles like "Bedroom package" (4 receptacles, a switch, a light) that drop a whole room onto a bid at once. Three starter kits ship with it. A kit's items land as ordinary line items, each with costs frozen at that moment and each editable afterwards, so one room being slightly different is just an edit to that line. Quantities inside a kit are yours to set from experience — nothing works them out from room size or spacing rules.
- Added **Quick bid** — a fast way to price a job you already know the counts for, with no plan or takeoff involved. Name the bid, then it's just type, arrow, Enter: search an assembly, press Enter, it's on the bid. The search box keeps focus so you never touch the mouse, the quantity sticks between entries (six of these, three of those), and counting the same assembly again adds to its existing line instead of piling up duplicate rows. The bid price updates as you go. Repeating units — duplicate rooms and the like — use the same generator the full Bids screen has. Everything underneath is the bid structure already built, so a quick bid and a full bid are the same thing, priced the same way.
- Fixed garbled characters in a source file's comments, introduced by an earlier tooling mistake. No effect on how the app behaves.
- Numbers are now quicker and safer to edit everywhere in the app. Clicking or tabbing into any number — a rate, a percentage, a quantity, an hour count — selects what's there, so typing replaces it instead of you having to clear the box first. Fields that save on their own now also save when you press Enter, and pressing Escape abandons whatever you typed and puts the last saved number back without saving anything. When a number does save, the field flashes green for a second so you can see it landed rather than having to guess — this app is about getting numbers right, and you should never have to wonder whether one took.
- Added **Bids** — the layer where assemblies become an actual job with a price. Create a bid, give it a status (Draft, Active, Won or Lost) and the trades it covers, then search your assembly library and add what the job needs with quantities. The moment you add an assembly its costs are frozen onto the bid, so editing a material price or an assembly next week cannot quietly change a bid you already sent out. The running total shows materials, labor hours, direct cost, overhead and profit, ending in the bid price. Overhead and profit come from a company-wide setting that each bid can override on its own; the panel always says which one it is using. For repetitive work — hotel rooms, spec homes — build one unit's worth of line items, label it (e.g. "Room 101"), and generate as many numbered copies as you need in one go. Every copy prices identically to the one it came from, each stays independently editable, and the total keeps up automatically.
- Fixed a bug found while testing the new Bids screen: the "copy a unit" picker could come up blank and refuse to generate anything, because it read the list of units before that list had loaded.
- Added the **Assembly Builder** — the screen where Materials, Labor Rates and Modifiers come together into a reusable recipe like "Duplex receptacle standard". Search and add materials with quantities, pick which role does the work and how many hours it takes, switch on the job conditions that apply, and watch the cost update as you type. The preview shows materials, labor and direct cost, and will add overhead and profit once you choose a profit method — it never picks one for you, because markup and target margin give different prices. Eight starter recipes ship with it, each editable: changing one gives you your own copy and leaves the original for a fresh start later. New assemblies open with a suggested labor time based on what you're building (a receptacle suggests 0.75 hr, a panel 8 hr) rather than a blank or a zero, and the screen says plainly that the suggestion is a starting point rather than a verified figure.
- Fixed a bug in the new Assembly Builder before it shipped: saving a change to one field — say the name — would silently wipe the assembly's material list and reset which role does the work. Caught by the new tests.
- Added two new Library screens. **Labor Rates** lists what each role costs you per hour — Apprentice, Journeyman, Foreman/Master Electrician, Supervisor and Project Manager to start — and roles are your own, so you can rename them or add any you like. A role can be paid hourly or by salary; for a salary you enter the yearly figure and how many hours a year that actually covers, and the app works out the hourly cost from those two numbers (the starter Project Manager comes out at $28.85/hr from $60,000 over 2,080 hours). Both numbers stay editable, because most shops bill fewer hours than a full payroll year and billing at 2,080 quietly under-recovers what that person costs. **Modifiers** lists job conditions that make work take longer — working at height, occupied buildings, overtime, night shift and weekends — as percentages you can tune. Percentages add rather than multiply, and the screen shows the combined effect on a 10-hour task so you can see it. Removing a modifier moves it to an Archived tab rather than deleting it, where you can put it back; deleting for good is only possible from there and asks first. All starter figures are placeholders to get you going, not researched market rates.
- Fixed a bug that could quietly duplicate the built-in starter lists. If two copies of the app started at the same moment, each could decide the starter items were missing and add its own set, leaving every item listed twice. Now only one can do the setup at a time, and any duplicates already created are cleaned up automatically on the next start.
- Materials can now be found by what electricians actually call them. Every starter material carries trade slang alongside its catalog name, so "1900" finds the 4" square box, "gem box" finds the single-gang, "romex" finds NM-B cable, "plug" finds the duplex receptacle, and "load center" or "breaker box" finds the main panel. Twelve common terms that previously returned nothing now work. Also fixed two ways search could return the wrong item: a two-letter manufacturer code was matching in the middle of unrelated words (searching "breaker" returned a single-gang box, because "ch" is inside "switch"), and searching a category name was pulling in everything filed under it.
- Fixed search ranking so the word you typed wins. Searching "recep" was putting "Wall plate" at the top, above "Duplex receptacle" — the search knows that outlet covers and wall plates are related, and that association was outranking the item you were actually looking for. Search still finds things by trade slang ("romex" finds NM-B cable), but those now sort below anything that genuinely matches what you typed. This affects every search box in the app, not just Materials.
- Materials now have a category, and the Materials screen is shelved by it — Wire & Cable, Conduit, Conduit Fittings, Boxes, Receptacles, Switches, Wall Plates & Misc, Panels & Breakers, Lighting Hardware — so you scroll to the section you want instead of hunting one long alphabetical list. All 28 starter materials were sorted into the right section automatically, including copies you had already edited.
