# Changelog

Plain-English record of what changed and when. Newest first.

This is the human-readable companion to the git history — read this to see what happened, read the commits for the technical detail.

## [2026-08-11]

- You can now change a labor rate straight from the Assembly Builder — a small pencil next to the role opens the rate right there, no trip to the Labor Rates screen. It says plainly that you are editing the one shared rate used across your whole library, not a rate for that assembly alone, and tells you how many assemblies will reprice. Bids you have already built keep the rates they were priced at.
- Fixed a serious bug this uncovered: editing one of the built-in labor roles quietly dropped the labor cost to **$0** on every assembly using it. Editing a built-in role makes your own copy of it, and assemblies were still pointing at the original — which then went into hiding, so nothing was found and the labor priced at nothing. Assemblies now follow the copy correctly. This affected assembly and kit costs; bids that had already frozen their costs were never at risk.
- Added the **Dashboard**, which replaces the old Projects page as the app's home view. It shows every bid in four columns — Draft, Active, Won, Lost — with each bid's name, value, deadline and line count on the card, so you can size up where everything stands without opening anything. Draft and Active sort by deadline with the soonest first, and bids with no deadline set drop to the bottom of those columns rather than crowding out the ones that have a real date. Won and Lost sort by whatever you touched most recently. Overdue and due-today deadlines are colour-coded, and the header shows what you have out for bid in total. **Bids now have a due date** — optional, since not every job has a firm deadline — which you set from the bid itself. Clicking any card opens that bid.
- Five changes that make building assemblies faster. **Small parts come with a sensible count** — add wire nuts and you get 3, not 1, because nobody fits a single one; same for straps and connectors, and every one is still just a starting number you can change. **Any assembly can be duplicated**, starter or your own, giving you a separate copy to modify — unlike editing a starter, which quietly makes it *your* version of that starter; a duplicate is its own thing and editing it never touches the original. **The materials you use most appear first** in the Assembly Builder before you type anything, so the same dozen parts are one click away. **The whole add-a-material flow works without the mouse**: type, arrow to pick, Enter to add, the quantity is waiting and selected, Enter again and you're back in the search box for the next one. **And there are now Kits** — named bundles like "Bedroom package" (4 receptacles, a switch, a light) that drop a whole room onto a bid at once. Three starter kits ship with it. A kit's items land as ordinary line items, each with costs frozen at that moment and each editable afterwards, so one room being slightly different is just an edit to that line. Quantities inside a kit are yours to set from experience — nothing works them out from room size or spacing rules.
- Added **Quick bid** — a fast way to price a job you already know the counts for, with no plan or takeoff involved. Name the bid, then it's just type, arrow, Enter: search an assembly, press Enter, it's on the bid. The search box keeps focus so you never touch the mouse, the quantity sticks between entries (six of these, three of those), and counting the same assembly again adds to its existing line instead of piling up duplicate rows. The bid price updates as you go. Repeating units — duplicate rooms and the like — use the same generator the full Bids screen has. Everything underneath is the bid structure already built, so a quick bid and a full bid are the same thing, priced the same way.
- Fixed garbled characters in a source file's comments, introduced by an earlier tooling mistake. No effect on how the app behaves.
- Numbers are now quicker and safer to edit everywhere in the app. Clicking or tabbing into any number — a rate, a percentage, a quantity, an hour count — selects what's there, so typing replaces it instead of you having to clear the box first. Fields that save on their own now also save when you press Enter, and pressing Escape abandons whatever you typed and puts the last saved number back without saving anything. When a number does save, the field flashes green for a second so you can see it landed rather than having to guess — this app is about getting numbers right, and you should never have to wonder whether one took.
- Added **Bids** — the layer where assemblies become an actual job with a price. Create a bid, give it a status (Draft, Active, Won or Lost) and the trades it covers, then search your assembly library and add what the job needs with quantities. The moment you add an assembly its costs are frozen onto the bid, so editing a material price or an assembly next week cannot quietly change a bid you already sent out. The running total shows materials, labor hours, direct cost, overhead and profit, ending in the bid price. Overhead and profit come from a company-wide setting that each bid can override on its own; the panel always says which one it is using. For repetitive work — hotel rooms, spec homes — build one unit's worth of line items, label it (e.g. "Room 101"), and generate as many numbered copies as you need in one go. Every copy prices identically to the one it came from, each stays independently editable, and the total keeps up automatically.
- Fixed a bug found while testing the new Bids screen: the "copy a unit" picker could come up blank and refuse to generate anything, because it read the list of units before that list had loaded.
- Added the **Assembly Builder** — the screen where Materials, Labor Rates and Modifiers come together into a reusable recipe like "Duplex receptacle standard". Search and add materials with quantities, pick which role does the work and how many hours it takes, switch on the job conditions that apply, and watch the cost update as you type. The preview shows materials, labor and direct cost, and will add overhead and profit once you choose a profit method — it never picks one for you, because markup and target margin give different prices. Eight starter recipes ship with it, each editable: changing one gives you your own copy and leaves the original for a fresh start later. New assemblies open with a suggested labor time based on what you're building (a receptacle suggests 0.75 hr, a panel 8 hr) rather than a blank or a zero, and the screen says plainly that the suggestion is a starting point rather than a verified figure.
- Fixed a bug in the new Assembly Builder before it shipped: saving a change to one field — say the name — would silently wipe the assembly's material list and reset which role does the work. Caught by the new tests.
- Added two new Library screens. **Labor Rates** lists what each role costs you per hour — Apprentice, Journeyman, Foreman/Master Electrician, Supervisor and Project Manager to start — and roles are your own, so you can rename them or add any you like. A role can be paid hourly or by salary; for a salary you enter the yearly figure and how many hours a year that actually covers, and the app works out the hourly cost from those two numbers (the starter Project Manager comes out at $28.85/hr from $60,000 over 2,080 hours). Both numbers stay editable, because most shops bill fewer hours than a full payroll year and billing at 2,080 quietly under-recovers what that person costs. **Modifiers** lists job conditions that make work take longer — working at height, occupied buildings, overtime, night shift and weekends — as percentages you can tune. Percentages add rather than multiply, and the screen shows the combined effect on a 10-hour task so you can see it. Removing a modifier moves it to an Archived tab rather than deleting it, where you can put it back; deleting for good is only possible from there and asks first. All starter figures are placeholders to get you going, not researched market rates.
- Fixed a bug that could quietly duplicate the built-in starter lists. If two copies of the app started at the same moment, each could decide the starter items were missing and add its own set, leaving every item listed twice. Now only one can do the setup at a time, and any duplicates already created are cleaned up automatically on the next start.
- Materials can now be found by what electricians actually call them. Every starter material carries trade slang alongside its catalog name, so "1900" finds the 4" square box, "gem box" finds the single-gang, "romex" finds NM-B cable, "plug" finds the duplex receptacle, and "load center" or "breaker box" finds the main panel. Twelve common terms that previously returned nothing now work. Also fixed two ways search could return the wrong item: a two-letter manufacturer code was matching in the middle of unrelated words (searching "breaker" returned a single-gang box, because "ch" is inside "switch"), and searching a category name was pulling in everything filed under it.
- Fixed search ranking so the word you typed wins. Searching "recep" was putting "Wall plate" at the top, above "Duplex receptacle" — the search knows that outlet covers and wall plates are related, and that association was outranking the item you were actually looking for. Search still finds things by trade slang ("romex" finds NM-B cable), but those now sort below anything that genuinely matches what you typed. This affects every search box in the app, not just Materials.
- Materials now have a category, and the Materials screen is shelved by it — Wire & Cable, Conduit, Conduit Fittings, Boxes, Receptacles, Switches, Wall Plates & Misc, Panels & Breakers, Lighting Hardware — so you scroll to the section you want instead of hunting one long alphabetical list. All 28 starter materials were sorted into the right section automatically, including copies you had already edited. Searching still looks across every category at once and lists the best matches first, with each result showing which section it came from. New materials you add can be filed on any shelf, or left unfiled.
- Planned out three ways to speed up counting items on a plan: a stamp tool that lets you pick an assembly once and click to drop it as many times as needed, AI that can drop those same pins for you automatically, and the ability to click a symbol in the legend to load its assembly instead of searching by name. Planning only — none of it is built yet.
- Decided how labor hours will work: they come from your own field experience with the NECA manual as background reading, and the existing Modifiers (height, outdoor, retrofit and so on) already cover what NECA handles with its Normal/Difficult/Very Difficult columns — so the app will not get a separate difficulty setting that asks the same question twice.
- The Materials library now has a real screen, reachable from the sidebar. It lists the 28 starter materials with their prices, lets you search by trade slang ("recep" finds receptacles), add your own, and edit any of them inline. Editing a starter material quietly hands you your own copy, marked "Your copy", with an undo button that puts the original back.
- Fixed `pnpm dev` on Windows — it was failing immediately with a "NODE_ENV is not recognized" error, so the app could not be started at all.
- Connected the new Materials library to the app. Editing one of the shipped starter materials now quietly gives you your own copy to change, leaving the original intact for everyone else, and you can undo back to the original at any time. Duplicate material names are refused, and prices are checked before saving. Still no screen — that's the last piece.
- Started building the new Materials library. This is the behind-the-scenes half: the app now ships with the 28 starter materials pre-loaded, and there's a working system for a contractor to take one of those shipped materials, make their own edited copy of it, and later undo their edits back to the original. Materials that are in use get hidden rather than deleted, so nothing an assembly points at can disappear. No screen for any of this yet — that comes next.
- Wrote out the parts list for all 27 first-tier starter assemblies — what materials go into a receptacle, a 3-way switch, an EV charger circuit, and so on. Labor hours are deliberately left blank; those come from the NECA manual. Also flagged 36 materials the assemblies need that aren't in the starter materials list yet, so they can be added with real supplier pricing.
