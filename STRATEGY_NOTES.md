# Strategy Notes

Planning document. Captures the strategic case for the product direction laid out in [ASSEMBLIES_PLAN.md](ASSEMBLIES_PLAN.md). No code changes.

## WHY THIS SURVIVES

### Market research (2026)

- Manual electrical estimating takes **6–12 hours per commercial bid** vs **2–3 hours** with purpose-built software.
- **73% of buyers** in this software category are **solo operators or 2–5 person shops** — not enterprise.
- Named competitor pain points:
  - **Duplicate entry** between separate takeoff and estimating tools.
  - **Tool fragmentation** — contractors stacking 3 separate tools (field service + commercial estimating + takeoff), costing **$100K+/year** combined, with poor data sharing between them.

### Case for

- The **integrated design directly solves** the duplicate-entry and tool-fragmentation problems.
- Built for the **underserved small-shop majority** rather than competing head-on with enterprise incumbents (Trimble, McCormick).
- The **labor-unit learning system** and **per-trade unlock pricing model** appear to be genuine gaps — not found in researched competitors.

### Case against

- Mature market with **entrenched incumbents** — McCormick (45+ years), ConEst (500,000+ assembly database).
- The full "everything in one app" vision (estimating + invoicing + billing) **risks spreading too thin** if attempted all at once.
- **Trust in bidding software is slow to earn.**

### Verdict

A **focused, genuinely excellent electrical estimating tool** for the underserved small-shop majority is the right starting point — **not** the full all-in-one vision at once.

**Validate with real outside users before expanding scope.**
